ONGOING Research 2026-Present

Evaluating Green Roofs as Living Bio-Filters: A High-Throughput eDNA Analysis of Stormwater Runoff Dynamics and Urban Watershed Health

Although there has been recent metabarcoding research on static microbiomes in green roofs’ substrate soils (like essential nitrogen-fixing and robust taxa), few studies have explored profiles of eDNA present in the liquid runoff during rain. We seek to answer the question: what is the impact of the green roof system on stormwater runoff in terms of its microbial community composition, species diversity, and indicator bacteria presence in comparison with that of conventional impervious surfaces and reference water bodies?

The goal is to eventually create a green roof model that takes the beneficial non-pathogenic soil taxa identified in 16S sequencing and intentionally inoculate a soil matrix to accelerate active biofiltration. Through hard physical metrics (CFU counts, turbidity, and volume retention) the green infrastructure should successfully intercept stormwater and prevent urban sewage (CSO) pollution.

Research Paper 2025-2026

Integrating Computational Modeling and Patient Data to Reveal Regulatory Pathways in Alzheimer’s Disease Neuroinflammation

View PDF Document ↗

Alzheimer's disease is a progressive neurodegenerative disorder with two prominent features: chronic neuroinflammation and the presence of Aβ plaques. It is known that several genetic risk factors, such as TREM2, IL1B, and ABCA1, can affect the risk of developing AD; however, our understanding of the complex interactions within the broader neuroinflammatory system remains unclear. This study addresses how neuron-glia interactions contribute to neuroinflammation in AD and how a Boolean model can identify key regulatory pathways. We hypothesize that computational simulations will reflect known biological outcomes, such as reduced immune activation after TREM2 knockout or increased Aβ buildup after ABCA1 loss. A qualitative Boolean network model of neuroinflammatory signaling was constructed in GINsim (v3.0.0). The model’s logical rules were built using gene expression data from published AD mouse knockout studies (TREM2, IL1B, ABCA1, APOE) and literature review. This computational model was then combined with human patient biomarker and gene expression data from the ADNI and AMP-AD databases to link simulations to clinical disease progression. The Boolean neuroinflammatory network model predicts sustained activation of microglial and pro-inflammatory nodes, along with reduced neuronal maintenance node activity, reflecting the neuroinflammatory patterns observed in Alzheimer’s disease. We performed in silico knockouts (KOs) by setting target nodes to 0 and analyzing the resulting states, or “attractors,” which represent long-term cellular conditions. Our results showed that simulated gene knockouts produced distinct outcomes. The absence of ABCA1 has resulted in a dramatic increase in glial-cell activation markers (such as GFAP and TREM2), indicating that ABCA1 generally inhibits the activation of glial cells. The absence of IL1B results in reduced levels of several inflammatory markers, further supporting its role as a principal mediator of inflammation.

Research Paper 2025-2026

Factors affecting Avian Biodiversity in Long Island Saltmarshes

  • UBRP Project Link ↗

    • Using data from this year, previous UBRP years, and eBird, our group assessed what prevalent bird species are in the Oceanside, Long Island, Marine Nature Study Area during the fall migration period and made inferences as to how these species' migratory patterns have changed over time. First, using feathers collected from the salt marsh, wooded, and dune areas of the Oceanside, NY Marine Nature Study Area. We learned how to understand different forms of DNA extraction for bird feathers. Then, we isolated the feather DNA using a PCR extraction method, checked if our PCR was successful using gel electrophoresis, and sent the DNA for sequencing. After receiving our sequenced DNA, we searched for our collected bird species using DNA Subway. Once our group had determined a list of species, we looked at past data involving migratory birds in the Marine Nature Study Area during the fall to early winter period.
